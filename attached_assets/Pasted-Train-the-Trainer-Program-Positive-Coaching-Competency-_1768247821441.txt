Training Philosophy
To ensure every employee is trained to perform tasks correctly, confidently, and independently, while demonstrating integrity, accountability, and comprehension.

Hip-to-Hip / Shadowing

Trainer stays alongside trainee throughout the process

Hands-On, Not Observational

Active participation, not just watching

Critical-Point Driven

Focus on safety, quality, and compliance checkpoints

Confidence Before Independence

Build competence before releasing trainee

Traceable & Repeatable

Documented and consistent across all departments

The 4-Step Training Model
This method applies uniformly, regardless of department, complexity, or role. Each step builds on the previous to ensure comprehensive competency.


1
Trainer Does / Trainer Explains

Introduce the task and establish correct mental models.


2
Trainer Does / Trainee Explains

Verify comprehension before hands-on execution.


3
Trainee Does / Trainer Coaches

Build confidence while preventing bad habits.


4
Trainee Does / Trainer Observes

Validate independent competence.

S-O-A Coaching Model
The S-O-A framework ensures feedback is constructive, specific, and action-oriented. Use this model for all coaching conversations.

S
Strength
"What did the trainee do well?"

Identify and reinforce correct behaviors. Build confidence first.

O
Opportunity
"What could be improved or refined?"

Frame mistakes as learning opportunities, not failures.

A
Action
"What will we do differently next time?"

Define specific, actionable next steps.

Example S-O-A Feedback:

"Your material prep was spot-on. One opportunity is checking orientation earlier to avoid rework. Next time, let's pause and verify before cutting."

Coaching Fundamentals
Teaching vs Coaching

Teaching	Coaching
Explains how	Develops judgment
Gives answers	Asks questions
Focuses on steps	Focuses on understanding
Psychological Safety

Trainees must feel safe to:

Ask questions freely
Admit uncertainty
Stop the process when unsure
But also understand:

Standards do not change
Critical points are not optional

Prohibited Behaviors During Training
The following behaviors are explicitly prohibited during training. Discipline belongs outside the training process and follows formal HR policy—not trainer discretion.

Yelling or raised voice

Public embarrassment

Sarcasm or ridicule

"Figure it out" responses

Withholding help to "test" someone

Trainer Escalation Guidance

If a trainee repeats errors, appears disengaged, or struggles with comprehension: Pause the process → Return to an earlier step → Document observations → Escalate to supervisor without judgment.
