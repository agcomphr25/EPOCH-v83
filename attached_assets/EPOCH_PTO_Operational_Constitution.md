# EPOCH PTO OPERATIONAL CONSTITUTION

**Document Classification:** Internal Policy — Authoritative  
**Effective Date:** April 29, 2026  
**Supersedes:** All prior verbal, written, or informal PTO approval practices  
**Maintained By:** Human Resources, in coordination with Payroll and Executive Leadership  
**Review Cycle:** Annual, or upon material change to regulatory or operational requirements

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This document establishes the official Paid Time Off (PTO) Operational Constitution for the company, governing all aspects of PTO administration within the EPOCH system. It replaces all prior informal approval practices — verbal authorizations, text message approvals, handwritten notes, and hallway agreements — which are hereby declared null and void as instruments of PTO approval.

EPOCH is designated as the single, authoritative system of record for all PTO requests, approvals, denials, cancellations, and payroll integrations. No PTO event is valid unless it is documented and processed within EPOCH.

This constitution establishes:

- A formal ownership model defining accountability at every stage of the PTO lifecycle
- A three-stage approval chain with defined SLAs and escalation paths
- Strict payroll governance rules, including a defined freeze cutoff
- A documented exception framework that eliminates undocumented deviations
- Audit and compliance standards consistent with DCAA requirements
- Enforcement expectations and disciplinary consequences for non-compliance

This document is intended for use by HR in policy enforcement, payroll in processing governance, supervisors in understanding approval accountability, and executive leadership in audit defense. It is a living constitutional standard — not a casual recommendation.

## LEADERSHIP POLICY STATEMENT

Effective immediately, the following policy applies to all employees, supervisors, department heads, payroll personnel, and owners operating within this organization:

**PTO that is not submitted, reviewed, and approved within EPOCH does not exist for payroll, compliance, or audit purposes.**

We have operated under informal approval practices that are no longer defensible — not for our employees, not for our payroll integrity, and not for our obligations as a government contractor subject to DCAA audit standards. The era of verbal sign-offs, text message approvals, and post-it note authorizations is over.

EPOCH is now the required workflow. It is not optional. It is not a suggestion. It is the system through which all PTO must flow, from initial request to payroll finalization.

We are making this change because we owe it to our employees to manage their time off with the same professionalism we apply to every other business operation. We owe it to our contracts to maintain records that can withstand external scrutiny. And we owe it to the integrity of our payroll to ensure that every approved hour of leave is documented, traceable, and reversible if necessary.

Leadership at every level is expected to model and enforce compliance with this constitution. Supervisors who fail to act on pending requests, payroll staff who process undocumented adjustments, and owners who bypass the system without documentation will be held accountable under the enforcement standards defined in Section 9 of this document.

This is policy. It is in effect now.

## SECTION 1 — POLICY STATEMENT

### 1.1 Governing Principle

EPOCH is the sole system of record for all PTO activity within this organization. All PTO requests, approvals, denials, cancellations, reversals, and payroll integrations must be initiated, processed, and finalized within EPOCH.

### 1.2 Invalid Approval Methods

The following are explicitly **not** recognized as valid PTO approvals under any circumstances:

- Verbal authorization from any person, including owners, executives, or supervisors
- Approval communicated via text message, SMS, or personal messaging application
- Approval communicated via email that has not been entered into EPOCH
- Handwritten notes, physical forms, or physical signatures
- Retroactive verbal confirmation of a prior informal arrangement
- Calendar entries or scheduling tool approvals made outside EPOCH

Any PTO event processed through any of the above methods, without corresponding documentation in EPOCH, is considered undocumented and will not be recognized by payroll.

### 1.3 System Authority

EPOCH is the authoritative source of truth for:

- Whether a PTO request has been submitted
- Whether a PTO request has been approved
- The scope, duration, and dates of approved leave
- The payroll treatment of approved leave
- The audit history of all PTO events

No person, role, or authority supersedes EPOCH's record. If EPOCH does not contain a record of the PTO event, the event did not occur for compliance and payroll purposes.

### 1.4 Mandatory Participation

All employees with access to EPOCH are required to submit PTO requests through the system. All supervisors with approval authority in EPOCH are required to process requests within the timelines defined in Section 3. All payroll personnel are required to use EPOCH as the basis for all PTO-related payroll decisions.

Exceptions to mandatory participation are governed exclusively by Section 7 of this document.

## SECTION 2 — PTO OWNERSHIP MODEL

### 2.1 Ownership Philosophy

Every stage of the PTO lifecycle has a designated owner. Ownership means accountability — not merely permission to act, but responsibility to act within defined timelines and standards.

### 2.2 Employee Responsibilities

The employee owns the PTO request. Specifically, the employee is responsible for:

- Submitting all PTO requests in EPOCH prior to taking leave, except in same-day illness or emergency situations as defined in Section 4
- Submitting requests with sufficient advance notice as defined in Section 4
- Providing documentation when required under Section 4
- Monitoring request status and following up with their supervisor if no action is taken within the SLA window defined in Section 3
- Canceling requests promptly if plans change, before payroll lock

The employee is not responsible for approval, but is responsible for ensuring their request exists in the system. An employee who takes leave without a submitted request bears full accountability for the resulting payroll discrepancy.

### 2.3 Supervisor Responsibilities

The supervisor owns first-level approval. Specifically, the direct supervisor is responsible for:

- Reviewing all submitted PTO requests within the SLA defined in Section 3
- Approving or denying requests with documented rationale
- Escalating to HR when the request requires second-level review
- Notifying HR when they are unavailable for an extended period so a delegate can be assigned
- Never approving PTO verbally or informally in lieu of EPOCH action
- Documenting the business reason for any denial in EPOCH at the time of denial

A supervisor who fails to act on a request within the defined SLA window triggers the escalation process defined in Section 3.

### 2.4 HR Responsibilities

HR owns second-level approval and policy governance. Specifically, HR is responsible for:

- Completing second-stage review within the SLA defined in Section 3
- Reviewing denial documentation for compliance with policy
- Managing delegate assignments when supervisors are unavailable
- Processing supervisor reassignments when organizational changes affect approval chains
- Maintaining this constitution and issuing annual updates
- Conducting periodic compliance audits of PTO records in EPOCH
- Owning all exception documentation as defined in Section 7

### 2.5 VP Responsibilities

The VP owns final approval authority. Specifically, the VP is responsible for:

- Completing third-stage final approval within the SLA defined in Section 3
- Acting as the escalation authority when lower-level approvers fail to act
- Approving exception requests submitted under Section 7
- Reviewing any request that has been escalated due to SLA breach

The VP may not delegate final approval authority to a non-management employee.

### 2.6 Payroll Responsibilities

Payroll owns the translation of approved PTO into payroll action. Specifically, payroll is responsible for:

- Processing only PTO events that have a complete, EPOCH-documented approval chain
- Refusing to process any PTO-related payroll adjustment that lacks EPOCH documentation
- Flagging discrepancies between approved PTO records and submitted timesheets
- Enforcing the payroll freeze cutoff defined in Section 5
- Initiating correction workflows when post-payroll adjustments are required

Payroll does not have authority to approve PTO. Payroll's role is execution of approved, documented leave.

### 2.7 Owner / Admin Override Authority

Owners and administrators retain the authority to override PTO decisions in exceptional circumstances. However, this authority is not exempt from documentation requirements. Specifically:

- Any owner-initiated override must be entered into EPOCH with a written rationale
- Owner overrides must be logged in the audit trail with the owner's identity and timestamp
- Owner overrides that occur after payroll lock must follow the correction workflow in Section 7
- No owner may instruct payroll to process undocumented PTO

The existence of override authority does not create an exemption from audit requirements.

## SECTION 3 — APPROVAL CHAIN STANDARD

### 3.1 Three-Stage Approval Chain

All PTO requests submitted through EPOCH follow a defined three-stage approval chain. A request is not approved until all required stages are complete.

**Stage 1 — Direct Supervisor Review**  
The employee's direct supervisor receives notification upon request submission. The supervisor must approve, deny, or escalate within the SLA defined in Section 3.3.

**Stage 2 — HR Review**  
Upon supervisor approval, HR receives the request for secondary review. HR must confirm or return the request within the SLA defined in Section 3.3. HR review ensures policy compliance, conflict checking, and classification accuracy.

**Stage 3 — VP Final Approval**  
Upon HR completion, the request is forwarded to the VP for final authorization. VP approval constitutes the completion of the approval chain and renders the PTO event payroll-eligible.

### 3.2 Approval Deadlines

Approvers must respond within the following windows, measured in business days from the date the request enters their queue:

- **Direct Supervisor:** 2 business days
- **HR:** 2 business days from supervisor completion
- **VP:** 1 business day from HR completion

For requests covering leave dates within 3 business days of submission, all approvers must complete their review within 1 business day.

### 3.3 Escalation for Non-Action

If an approver fails to act within the defined deadline:

1. EPOCH generates an automated escalation notification to the approver and their superior
2. If no action is taken within 1 additional business day after escalation, HR is notified
3. HR may reassign the request to an available approver or escalate to the VP
4. A record of the non-action and escalation is preserved in the audit trail

Failure to act on a request is itself a compliance failure and is subject to enforcement under Section 9.

### 3.4 Denial Standards

Any denial must include:

- The specific business or policy reason for the denial
- The approver's identity and timestamp
- Whether the employee may resubmit for different dates

Denials issued without documented rationale are considered incomplete and may be returned by HR for reprocessing.

### 3.5 Delegate Handling

When a supervisor or approver will be unavailable for more than 2 consecutive business days, they must:

1. Notify HR in advance
2. Designate a qualified delegate with equivalent or greater approval authority
3. Ensure the delegate assignment is recorded in EPOCH

Pending requests are not paused during unavailability. The delegate assumes full approval authority and accountability.

### 3.6 Supervisor Reassignment

When an employee's direct supervisor changes due to organizational restructuring, termination, promotion, or reassignment, HR must update the approval chain in EPOCH within 1 business day of the change taking effect. Requests pending at the time of reassignment must be actioned by the newly assigned supervisor within the standard SLA window.

## SECTION 4 — PTO REQUEST RULES

### 4.1 Recognized Leave Types

The following leave types are recognized within EPOCH:

- **Full Day** — Complete absence for a standard workday
- **Half Day** — Absence for the first or second half of a standard workday
- **Hourly / Partial Day** — Absence for a specific number of hours less than a half day
- **Multi-Day** — Consecutive or non-consecutive leave spanning multiple calendar days submitted as a single request

### 4.2 Advance Notice Requirements

Unless otherwise specified below, PTO requests must be submitted no later than:

- **Vacation and Planned Leave:** Minimum 5 business days in advance
- **Floating Holiday:** Minimum 2 business days in advance
- **Partial Day / Hourly Leave:** Minimum 1 business day in advance, or at the start of the scheduled workday when operationally necessary

These are minimum requirements. Employees are encouraged to submit requests as early as possible, particularly for extended leave.

### 4.3 Same-Day Illness

When an employee is unable to report to work due to illness and does not have a pre-submitted request, the following protocol applies:

1. The employee must notify their direct supervisor as early as possible, before or at the start of the scheduled shift
2. The employee or the supervisor must submit a PTO request in EPOCH on the same day or the next business day, whichever is the earliest practicable opportunity
3. Documentation is not required for single-day illness absences unless the employee has an existing attendance pattern under HR review
4. For illness absences exceeding 3 consecutive days, HR may require documentation at its discretion

### 4.4 Bereavement Leave

Bereavement leave is handled through EPOCH using the bereavement leave type. The following standards apply:

- Employees are not required to submit a bereavement request before the start of leave; submission must occur within 1 business day of return
- HR may request documentation (obituary, death certificate) at its discretion
- Bereavement leave does not consume PTO accrual where a separate bereavement benefit exists; HR will classify the leave appropriately in EPOCH

### 4.5 Floating Holiday

Floating holidays are submitted as a standard leave request and must be designated as the floating holiday type in EPOCH. Floating holiday leave is subject to the same approval chain as standard PTO.

### 4.6 Emergency Leave

Emergency leave (non-illness, non-bereavement) must be submitted in EPOCH as soon as practicable. If the emergency prevents same-day submission, the employee or a designated contact must notify the supervisor verbally, and the request must be entered in EPOCH within 1 business day of the employee's return.

Emergency leave does not waive documentation or approval requirements. The emergency context must be noted in the request.

### 4.7 Manager-Submitted Requests

A supervisor may submit a PTO request on behalf of an employee in the following circumstances only:

- The employee is unable to access EPOCH due to a documented system issue
- The employee is incapacitated and unable to submit personally
- The request covers retroactive leave being corrected after a system cutover

Manager-submitted requests must be flagged as such in EPOCH and must include a note explaining the reason for proxy submission. The employee must be notified of the proxy submission as soon as practicable.

### 4.8 Documentation Requirements

Documentation is required for:

- Medical leave exceeding 3 consecutive days
- Leave designated as FMLA or equivalent statutory leave
- Bereavement leave (at HR discretion)
- Military leave (federal orders required)
- Jury duty (summons or service confirmation required)

Documentation is not required for:

- Standard planned vacation leave
- Floating holiday use
- Single-day illness leave (unless flagged by HR)
- Emergency leave (documentation of emergency context in EPOCH notes is sufficient)

## SECTION 5 — TIMESHEET + PAYROLL GOVERNANCE

### 5.1 Integration of Approved PTO into Payroll

Only PTO that has completed the full three-stage approval chain defined in Section 3 is eligible for payroll processing. Approved PTO in EPOCH is treated as follows:

**Salaried Employees:** Approved PTO is recorded as paid leave for the applicable period. No reduction to gross pay occurs for approved leave. Unapproved absence may be subject to pay adjustment per HR determination.

**Hourly Employees:** Approved PTO is coded as paid leave hours equal to the employee's standard scheduled hours for the absent period. Approved PTO hours are included in total payable hours for the pay period and classified as indirect labor unless a specific charge code applies.

### 5.2 Payroll Export and Labor Costing

EPOCH generates a payroll export that maps approved PTO records to the appropriate payroll codes. Payroll must use this export as the authoritative source for PTO-related entries.

PTO hours are classified as indirect labor for government contract labor costing purposes and must not be allocated to direct contract charge codes. Any deviation from this classification requires explicit HR and management authorization and must be documented in EPOCH.

### 5.3 Prohibited Payroll Actions

The following actions are explicitly prohibited:

- Processing pay for hours where an employee both clocked in for productive work and claimed PTO — double-coding of the same hours is prohibited
- Bypassing the EPOCH export to manually enter PTO data into the payroll system without corresponding EPOCH records
- Adjusting payroll to reflect PTO that was not approved through EPOCH's approval chain
- Retroactively inserting PTO records into EPOCH after payroll has been finalized without initiating the formal correction workflow defined in Section 7

### 5.4 DCAA Labor Charging Compliance

For purposes of DCAA audit defensibility, all PTO hours must be:

- Documented in EPOCH prior to or contemporaneously with the leave event
- Classified correctly as indirect labor
- Supported by a complete approval chain record in EPOCH's audit log
- Free of any indication that the employee performed direct contract work during claimed PTO hours

Any timesheet that shows contract work hours and approved PTO hours for the same time period will be flagged for HR and management review before payroll finalization.

### 5.5 Payroll Freeze Cutoff

**Payroll Lock Rule:** No new PTO approvals may be processed into a finalized payroll period once the payroll lock date has been reached. The payroll lock date is defined as the close of business on the last business day before the payroll processing run begins for each period.

Requests submitted or approved after the lock date that apply to the current pay period must be processed as a payroll correction in the following period, following the correction workflow defined in Section 7.

The payroll lock date for each period will be communicated by Payroll to all supervisors and HR no later than 5 business days before the lock date.

## SECTION 6 — CANCELLATION + REVERSAL POLICY

### 6.1 Employee Self-Cancellation

An employee may cancel a submitted PTO request at any time before it enters the VP approval stage, provided:

- The cancellation is submitted in EPOCH by the employee
- The cancellation is made before the start date of the leave
- No payroll action has been taken on the request

After VP approval has been granted, self-cancellation requires supervisor acknowledgment before it is finalized in EPOCH.

### 6.2 Admin Cancellation

An administrator or HR officer may cancel an approved PTO request on the employee's behalf in the following circumstances:

- The employee has requested the cancellation and is unable to perform it themselves
- The approved leave conflicts with a company-declared mandatory attendance event, with appropriate notice to the employee
- The original approval was issued in error (incorrect date, classification, or duration)

All admin cancellations must include a written rationale entered into EPOCH at the time of the action.

### 6.3 Post-Approval Reversal Standards

A reversal of previously approved PTO (after the leave event has begun or concluded) requires:

- Written rationale entered into EPOCH by the initiating party
- HR review and sign-off
- Payroll determination of whether a correction is required in the current or subsequent pay period
- Notification to the employee in writing

Reversals may not be performed by the employee unilaterally once the leave event has begun. Reversals that affect finalized payroll follow the correction workflow defined in Section 7.

### 6.4 Authority to Reverse Approved PTO

The following parties may initiate a reversal, subject to the conditions above:

- HR (in all cases, with documented rationale)
- The original approving VP (for operational corrections)
- Payroll (to initiate a correction workflow when a payroll discrepancy is identified)

No other party may reverse an approved PTO record without escalating through HR.

### 6.5 Audit Trail Requirement

Every cancellation and reversal — without exception — must produce a complete audit trail entry in EPOCH including:

- The identity of the person initiating the action
- The timestamp of the action
- The reason for the action
- The prior status of the record and the resulting status after action
- Whether a payroll correction was triggered

Silent reversals — changes to PTO records made without audit trail documentation — are prohibited and constitute a compliance violation.

## SECTION 7 — EXCEPTION HANDLING

### 7.1 Governing Principle

Exceptions to the standard PTO workflow exist. Operational reality, human circumstances, and statutory obligations create situations that the standard process cannot fully anticipate. However, the existence of an exception never eliminates the documentation requirement. All exceptions must be documented in EPOCH.

**No undocumented exception is permitted. This is an absolute rule.**

### 7.2 Owner Bypass Scenarios

When an owner or executive requests or authorizes leave outside the standard approval chain, the following protocol applies:

1. The owner must submit or direct submission of the PTO event in EPOCH
2. The request must be flagged as an owner-authorized exception with a written explanation
3. HR must be notified within 1 business day and must acknowledge the exception in EPOCH
4. The exception is included in the audit trail under the owner's identity
5. If the exception affects a finalized payroll period, the correction workflow in Section 7.7 applies

Owner bypass does not mean owner exemption. Documentation is mandatory.

### 7.3 Urgent Travel and Business Emergency

When operational demands require an employee's last-minute departure or an executive-directed emergency travel arrangement that conflicts with standard PTO notice requirements:

- The PTO request must be submitted in EPOCH as soon as the departure is confirmed
- If departure precedes submission, the request must be entered within 1 business day of departure
- The request must note the emergency context
- Standard approval chain applies unless the timeline requires VP fast-track approval, which HR may authorize on a case-by-case basis

### 7.4 Emergency Family Situations

Personal family emergencies that prevent advance notice or same-day submission are handled as follows:

- Employee or designated contact notifies the supervisor verbally as early as possible
- PTO request is entered in EPOCH within 1 business day of the employee's return to work
- Documentation is not required for single emergency events
- For extended emergency leave, FMLA or equivalent statutory protections may apply; HR must be consulted

### 7.5 Military Leave

Military leave is governed by the Uniformed Services Employment and Reemployment Rights Act (USERRA) and applicable state equivalents. The following requirements apply:

- The employee must provide advance notice when military orders are known in advance
- A copy of the military orders must be submitted to HR and attached to the EPOCH leave request
- Military leave is classified separately from standard PTO and does not draw from the employee's PTO balance unless the employee elects to supplement military pay with PTO
- HR determines the payroll treatment of military leave in coordination with payroll

### 7.6 Jury Duty

Jury duty leave requires:

- A copy of the jury summons submitted to HR and attached to the EPOCH request
- The request must be submitted as soon as the summons is received
- Jury duty leave does not draw from PTO balance per company policy; HR will classify appropriately
- The employee must notify their supervisor daily during jury service when the leave will continue
- Upon completion of jury service, a copy of the service confirmation must be provided to HR

### 7.7 Post-Payroll Corrections

When a PTO event must be corrected after the payroll period has been finalized, the following correction workflow applies:

1. **Identification:** Payroll or HR identifies the discrepancy through audit, employee report, or system review
2. **Documentation:** The initiating party enters a correction request in EPOCH with full detail of the original event, the nature of the discrepancy, and the proposed correction
3. **HR Review:** HR reviews and approves the correction request within 2 business days
4. **Payroll Action:** Payroll processes the correction in the next available payroll cycle, or as an off-cycle adjustment if the correction is time-sensitive
5. **Audit Entry:** The correction, its authorization, and its payroll treatment are recorded in EPOCH's audit log

Post-payroll corrections may not be processed by payroll without a completed, HR-approved correction request in EPOCH.

### 7.8 Legacy Pre-Cutover Exceptions

For leave events that occurred prior to the implementation of EPOCH as the system of record and were handled through prior informal methods:

- HR must work with the employee and supervisor to reconstruct the event based on available records
- Reconstructed events must be entered into EPOCH as historical corrections, flagged as pre-cutover
- Supporting documentation (emails, text records, calendar entries) must be attached to the EPOCH record where available
- Payroll must review all reconstructed records for any outstanding adjustment obligations

Legacy exceptions are a time-limited category. No new leave events will be classified as legacy exceptions after a date designated by HR as the "cutover clean" date.

## SECTION 8 — AUDIT + COMPLIANCE STANDARD

### 8.1 DCAA Audit Requirements

As a government contractor, this organization is subject to Defense Contract Audit Agency (DCAA) review standards. PTO records are subject to audit as part of labor cost verification. To meet DCAA requirements, all PTO events must be:

- Documented contemporaneously with or prior to the leave event
- Approved through the documented chain of authority before being charged to any cost period
- Traceable from the original employee request through each stage of approval to final payroll treatment
- Free of retroactive adjustment unless processed through the formal correction workflow in Section 7.7
- Classifiable as indirect labor without ambiguity

DCAA auditors may request, and must be able to receive, full documentation of any PTO event including all approval stages, timestamps, identities of approvers, and payroll treatment.

### 8.2 Events That Must Be Reconstructable

The following events must produce a complete, reconstructable audit record in EPOCH:

| Event | Required Audit Information |
|---|---|
| PTO Request Submitted | Employee identity, submission timestamp, leave dates, leave type |
| Stage 1 Approval | Supervisor identity, approval timestamp, any notes |
| Stage 1 Denial | Supervisor identity, denial timestamp, documented reason |
| Stage 2 Review | HR reviewer identity, review timestamp, confirmation or return action |
| Stage 3 Approval | VP identity, final approval timestamp |
| Cancellation | Canceling party identity, timestamp, reason |
| Reversal | Initiating party identity, HR authorization, timestamp, reason, payroll impact |
| Override | Override authority identity, timestamp, reason |
| Payroll Export | Export timestamp, pay period, total hours included |
| Post-Payroll Correction | Correction request, HR approval, payroll processing confirmation |

### 8.3 Internal Ownership Review

In addition to DCAA compliance, EPOCH's audit records must support internal ownership review. Owners must be able to, at any time, review:

- All pending PTO requests and their current approval stage
- All approved PTO for any employee or time period
- All denials and the documented reasons
- All exceptions processed in the current year
- Payroll treatment of all approved leave

### 8.4 Audit Retention

All PTO records, audit trail entries, and associated documentation must be retained in EPOCH for a minimum of 7 years from the date of the leave event, consistent with DCAA record retention requirements and applicable labor law.

### 8.5 Periodic Internal Compliance Audit

HR will conduct an internal PTO compliance review not less than quarterly. The review will examine:

- Approval chain completion rates
- SLA adherence by stage
- Exception volume and documentation quality
- Payroll discrepancy rates
- Any instances of informal approval attempts

Results of each review will be reported to the VP and documented.

## SECTION 9 — ENFORCEMENT + DISCIPLINE

### 9.1 Governing Principle

System integrity is not subordinate to convenience. Non-compliance with this constitution — at any level of the organization — is a process failure with defined consequences. The enforcement framework below applies to all personnel without exception.

### 9.2 Employee Non-Compliance

When an employee takes leave without submitting a corresponding EPOCH request, the following applies:

- First Occurrence: Written notice from HR; the employee must retroactively document the event in EPOCH within 2 business days
- Second Occurrence: Formal written warning placed in the employee's personnel file; payroll holds the disputed hours pending HR review
- Third Occurrence and Beyond: Escalation to progressive discipline under the company's standard HR disciplinary process, up to and including termination

An employee who takes leave without EPOCH documentation and whose request cannot be retroactively completed and approved may have the absent hours treated as unexcused absence per HR determination.

### 9.3 Supervisor Non-Compliance

When a supervisor fails to act on a pending PTO request within the defined SLA window:

- First Occurrence: Automated escalation notification with copy to HR; the supervisor must act within 1 business day
- Second Occurrence (within 90 days): HR consultation with the supervisor regarding process adherence
- Pattern of Non-Compliance (3 or more SLA breaches in any 90-day window): Formal performance notation; review of approval authority

When a supervisor is found to have granted informal verbal or written approvals outside EPOCH:

- The supervisor must immediately document the event in EPOCH
- HR issues a formal written notice
- The informal approval is retroactively reviewed for whether it can be validated through EPOCH; if not, the leave may be reclassified

### 9.4 Payroll Non-Compliance

When payroll processes a PTO-related adjustment without corresponding EPOCH documentation:

- The adjustment must be reversed and reprocessed through the documented correction workflow
- HR and the VP are notified
- The responsible payroll staff member receives a formal written notice
- A corrected payroll run is initiated as soon as practicable

### 9.5 Owner / Executive Non-Compliance

When an owner or executive directs an undocumented PTO exception or instructs payroll to act without EPOCH documentation:

- HR must document the instruction and its context in EPOCH
- HR must notify the VP (or in the case of VP-level directive, the board or designated governance authority)
- The event is flagged in the compliance review record
- Payroll may not act on an undocumented owner directive; if instructed to do so, payroll must escalate to HR immediately

No person holds authority to override this constitution's documentation requirements. Owner authority includes the authority to authorize exceptions; it does not include authority to waive documentation.

### 9.6 Whistleblower Protections

Employees who report non-compliance with this constitution in good faith are protected from retaliation. Reports of non-compliance should be directed to HR. HR is required to log all compliance reports and their resolutions.

## SECTION 10 — FUTURE STATE

### 10.1 Current Operational State

As of the effective date of this constitution, EPOCH supports the following PTO functions:

- PTO request submission by employees
- Three-stage approval chain processing
- Payroll export integration for approved leave
- Audit trail logging for all PTO events
- Exception documentation workflows

EPOCH does not currently support automated PTO accrual calculations or real-time balance tracking. These functions are planned for a future release.

### 10.2 Planned Future Module: PTO Accrual + Balances

A PTO accrual and balance engine is planned as a future EPOCH module. This module will provide:

- Automated accrual of PTO hours based on employee classification, tenure, and company policy
- Real-time balance tracking visible to employees and supervisors
- Integration of balance data into the approval workflow to flag requests that exceed available balance
- Historical accrual reporting for audit purposes

**This module does not exist today.** Supervisors and HR must not assume that EPOCH currently enforces balance limits. PTO requests must be reviewed manually against available balances until the accrual module is implemented.

### 10.3 Transition Protocol

When the accrual module is implemented:

- A formal transition plan will be issued by HR
- Historical leave data will be reconciled against accrual records
- Employees will be notified of their opening balances and the method of calculation
- This constitution will be updated to reflect balance enforcement provisions

Until that transition occurs, all personnel should operate under the assumption that balance verification is a manual, human-owned responsibility.

### 10.4 Acknowledgment of Non-Automated State

Any assumption that PTO balances are currently enforced automatically by EPOCH is incorrect. System-enforced balance management is a future capability. This section exists to prevent payroll or approval decisions from being made on the basis of assumed automation that does not yet exist.

## PAYROLL FREEZE RECOMMENDATION

### Formal Freeze Cutoff Rule

**No PTO approval entered into EPOCH after the payroll lock date may be processed into the current pay period's payroll run.**

The payroll lock date is defined as the close of business on the last business day before the payroll processing run begins for each pay period.

### Implementation Requirements

1. **Calendar Publication:** Payroll must publish the lock date calendar for the full fiscal year no later than the first business day of each calendar year. Updates to the calendar require at least 10 business days advance notice to HR and all supervisors.

2. **Pre-Lock Reminder:** EPOCH should generate automated reminders to all pending approvers 48 hours before each lock date.

3. **Hard Lock Enforcement:** No exceptions to the lock cutoff may be made without joint approval from HR and the VP, with documentation entered into EPOCH before payroll is run.

4. **Post-Lock Requests:** Requests approved after the lock date for current-period leave are queued as pending corrections and processed in the immediately following pay period through the correction workflow in Section 7.7.

5. **Communication of Locked Requests:** Employees whose requests are approved after the lock date must be notified by HR within 1 business day, with an explanation of the correction timeline.

## EXCEPTION GOVERNANCE RULES

### Rule 1 — No Exception Without Documentation
Every exception to the standard PTO workflow must be documented in EPOCH before payroll action is taken on the exception. There are no documentation-exempt exceptions.

### Rule 2 — Exception Authority Is Defined and Limited
Exceptions may only be authorized by HR, the VP, or an Owner. No supervisor, department head, or payroll staff member may self-authorize an exception to this constitution.

### Rule 3 — Owner Bypass Exceptions Require HR Notification
Any exception initiated by an owner must be communicated to HR within 1 business day. HR must acknowledge the exception in EPOCH.

### Rule 4 — Exception Volume Is Monitored
HR will track the volume, type, and frequency of exceptions. High exception volume from any individual, team, or request type will trigger a process review.

### Rule 5 — Statutory Leave Exceptions Are Not Discretionary
Exceptions for military leave, jury duty, FMLA, and bereavement are governed by applicable law and company policy, not by manager preference. These must be classified correctly in EPOCH regardless of operational inconvenience.

### Rule 6 — Post-Payroll Exceptions Follow Correction Workflow
An exception that is identified after payroll finalization is not an exception from the correction workflow. All post-payroll exceptions follow the documented correction process in Section 7.7 without modification.

### Rule 7 — Legacy Exceptions Are Time-Bounded
Legacy pre-cutover exceptions are available only until HR declares the cutover clean date. After that date, no new events may be retroactively classified as legacy exceptions.

### Rule 8 — Exceptions Do Not Establish Precedent
An approved exception in one case does not create an entitlement to the same exception in a future case. Each exception is reviewed on its own merits under the standards of this constitution.

## DCAA COMPLIANCE NOTES

### Note 1 — System of Record Requirement
DCAA requires that labor charges, including indirect leave, be supported by a contemporaneous, system-of-record documentation trail. EPOCH serves as that system of record for PTO. All PTO events must be entered into EPOCH prior to payroll processing.

### Note 2 — Indirect Labor Classification
All PTO hours must be classified as indirect labor in EPOCH and in the payroll export. Direct contract charge codes may not include PTO hours without explicit, documented authorization and justification, which itself must be available for DCAA review.

### Note 3 — Prohibition on Double-Charging
DCAA strictly prohibits charging both productive labor hours and PTO hours to any cost period for the same employee. EPOCH must be used to enforce the absence of time-overlap between PTO records and productive work records.

### Note 4 — Contemporaneous Documentation Standard
DCAA auditors assess whether labor records were created at the time of the work event, not reconstructed later. All PTO documentation must be entered into EPOCH as close to the leave event as operationally possible. Retroactive entries must be clearly flagged and explained.

### Note 5 — Approval Chain as Audit Evidence
The complete approval chain record in EPOCH — including approver identities, timestamps, and stage-by-stage completion — constitutes the primary audit evidence for any DCAA review of PTO charges. This record must be complete and unaltered.

### Note 6 — Record Retention
PTO records must be retained for no less than 7 years from the date of the leave event, consistent with DCAA record retention requirements under FAR 4.703 and related provisions.

### Note 7 — Exception Documentation as Audit Evidence
All exceptions documented under Section 7 must be retained with the same rigor as standard PTO records. DCAA auditors may request exception records as part of a labor compliance review.

### Note 8 — Payroll Correction Auditability
Post-payroll corrections must be documentable from the original discrepancy identification through the correction workflow to final payroll treatment. A correction that cannot be fully traced is not audit-defensible.
